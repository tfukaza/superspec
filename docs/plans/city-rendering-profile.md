# Profiling the illustrated Three.js city

Phase one uses the published Stoa city in the classic Three.js WebGL viewer. It keeps TypeScript gameplay, the illustrated materials and the existing lighting. The immediate target is a 16.67 ms frame at 1920×1080 on the RTX 5060 Ti, with the first-person walk as the primary view. Use the overhead pan to stress the larger visible city. Evaluate a different rendering backend in phase two using the workload and fidelity requirements established here.

## Results of the rendering investigations

The classic WebGL viewer uses the adaptive visibility grid, compact geometry clustering, fixed-size viewport guard and early color-shader preparation. These address different workloads. The grid saves work during camera movement. Clustering and overlapping the first color compile with driver work shorten startup. See the [integrated 1080p checks](../../generated/runtime-performance/integrated-rendering/report.md) for the before/after viewer comparison and its limits.

On the RTX 5060 Ti, the final built viewer completed 205 FPS on the street and 69 overhead at 1080p. The two paired integration runs lost warmed throughput in both views. A separate paced replay averaged 55 FPS on both routes with an idle browser cadence of 60 FPS. The largest measured increase occurred from the first color draw to the end of that pass. The underlying cause remains open. [Route measurements and diagnostic reversals](../../generated/runtime-performance/integrated-rendering/report.md)

## Brief to give every investigator

> Find out whether your stage limits a frame, under which conditions, and how much time is recoverable. Start with your own map of the stage and its neighboring work. Look up how production engines or other renderers handle the same problem, link the primary sources you used, and explain what carries over to Three.js/WebGL. Bring competing explanations, including the possibility that another stage owns the delay. Choose a test whose outcomes distinguish those explanations and say what evidence would change your mind. The Stoa figures and experiments below are examples from particular runs. Replace the suggested probe or follow a lead across stages when the evidence warrants it. Return measured results, uncertainty, fidelity impact and any newly discovered bottleneck. Ruling out an expensive rewrite is a useful outcome.

For example, Unreal retains eligible mesh draw commands, whereas Unity's SRP Batcher keeps material constants resident across draws. These address different kinds of submission cost, even for different buildings that share parts. Godot's occlusion documentation describes when CPU occluders pay for themselves, which suggests testing scenes with different amounts of occlusion. Other starting points include isolating GPU pipeline stages by varying their work, and correlating CPU submission, waits and GPU execution on a single timeline. Each agent should extend the research in their own area. [Unreal mesh drawing](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/mesh-drawing-pipeline-in-unreal-engine) · [Unity SRP Batcher](https://docs.unity.com/en-us/engine/6000.0/manual/analysis/graphics-performance-profiling/in-urp/reduce-draw-calls-urp/srpbatcher/srpbatcher) · [Godot occlusion](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/3d/occlusion_culling.html) · [NVIDIA pipeline experiments](https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems/part-v-performance-and-practicalities/chapter-28-graphics-pipeline-performance) · [Microsoft timing captures](https://devblogs.microsoft.com/pix/timing-captures-new/)

## Shared measurement contract

1. Pin the viewer bundle, published GLB, Three.js version, Chrome version, driver, draw-buffer size and renderer. Use the normal illustrated WebGL path. Benchmark the same 720 poses for each of `spawn-walk` and `city-pan`. Record whether the route has already been traversed.
2. Use uncapped **GPU-completed throughput** for capacity and a separate **paced** trial for late frames. The paced harness measures animation-frame callbacks. Record the display refresh, median and p95 interval, and suitably late frames (such as over 25 ms on a 60 Hz display). Confirm actual presentation with a compositor trace when relevant. CPU wall time can include driver blocking, and GPU pass times overlap CPU work. A browser trace with CPU samples, queue submits, fences and presentation events can distinguish hot JavaScript from waits or unrelated main-thread work.
3. Agents can investigate source, use saved traces and build candidate bundles concurrently. Give one benchmark process the GPU at a time. Reverse control/candidate order, repeat in a fresh browser, and report control spread and paired differences. Keep fine-grained instrumentation out of the throughput run.
4. Save matched images from the street, the overhead view and a transition specific to the change. Record draw, triangle and instance counts, plus material and shadow state when relevant. For changes intended to preserve drawing exactly, compare per-pose work and images to a repeated control. For a fidelity trade, state precisely what was simplified and where.
5. Each owner returns their baseline **ms/frame**, counterfactual or candidate **ms saved**, distribution across runs, image or membership check, and the next decision the measurement supports. Report cold hitches and steady costs separately. Re-run the integrated build before adding savings from different investigations.

The existing CLI accepts `--renderer webgl --resolution 1920x1080 --views spawn-walk,city-pan --frames 720 --throughput`. Use `--paced` or `--passes` in independent runs. For a cold route select `--first-traversal --paced` in a separate invocation. Ordinary moving-view runs prime the whole route. Report separately which disk, browser and driver caches were fresh or unverified. ## Previous evidence in this workspace

The hidden-transform experiment is one example of a cost that was real in isolation and too small to produce a repeatable frame-rate gain. In two fresh launches its pooled results were 142.9 → 135.8 FPS street and 62.2 → 61.5 FPS overhead. The viewer retained the original implementation. The CPU figures above come from an earlier 720-pose WebGL profile and the GPU pass figures from another instrumented WebGL run. They describe different samples of the illustrated scene. ### Item 2 scene preparation

Use one scene preparation lifecycle for color, shadow and outline. A transform or attachment change marks its branch dirty. Flush those branches before culling or drawing uses them, and invalidate derived world-space bounds and occluders through the same lifecycle. Each pass still selects its own geometry, layers and order. Keep the one-shot outline transform skip as profiling evidence. It removed 0.235 ms on the street and 0.675 ms overhead from the matrix stage, with a wide spread in paired overhead frame time. A late LOD reattachment can also leave its descendants stale. [Hardware trial](../../generated/runtime-performance/outline-transform-reuse/summary.json)

The shared-preparation prototype replayed the authored Stoa graph with placeholder meshes and all 20 declared LOD chains. It updated only dirty branches and used Three's normal per-object matrix update. At the color, canonical shadow and outline boundaries, all attached world matrices, visibility flags and camera inverses matched the stock traversal across 120 frames per workload. The moving-parent replays changed translation, rotation and nonuniform scale, and introduced some LOD branches after the canonical shadow stage.

| CPU replay workload | Stock preparation | Shared preparation | Matrix multiplications per sampled frame |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Static city with moving camera and light target | 0.622 ms | 0.001 ms | 8,452 → 2 |
| LOD reattachment | 0.711 ms | 0.010 ms | 8,556 → 80 |
| Moving a group of LOD placements | 0.838 ms | 0.025 ms | 8,556 → 161 |
| Moving the entire city | 0.876 ms | 0.466 ms | 8,556 → 4,301 |

Times are medians of warmed 180-frame periods for scene mutations and matrix updates. The replay models two stock matrix walks per frame, with shadows consuming the prepared color transforms. Its LOD decisions follow a predetermined sequence. Browser frame time, runtime-generated batches, GPU work and the identities of queued draws are outside this measurement. [Replay and raw samples](../../generated/runtime-performance/outline-transform-reuse/shared-transforms.json) · [Replay driver](../../generated/runtime-performance/outline-transform-reuse/replay-shared-transforms.mjs)

A test using CityVisibility reproduced a stale-bound rejection after a branch moved into the frustum with correct matrices. Another using Three's WebGL render list reversed two fixed transparent draws by moving the camera. A shared scene can provide current transforms and bounds while retaining pass-specific admission and view-dependent ordering. The saved staging profile puts overhead list construction and geometry updates at 1.41 ms and sorting at 0.13 ms. Outline list construction alone was 0.21 ms overhead and 0.055 ms on the street. These measurements favor improving the common scene preparation over caching an outline-specific list.
